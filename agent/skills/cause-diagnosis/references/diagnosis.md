# Distinguishing causes of an apparent failure

A click intended to select a tile changes nothing:

- The recorded click lies outside the tile: first repair target grounding. The observation does not test the tile's behavior.
- The recorded click lies inside it, but the tile is covered: selection and occlusion remain separate uncertainties. Inspect the action's intermediate frames or a visible response indicator.
- The tile changes only after another input: delayed selection and the second input's direct effect both fit. Isolate one factor before modifying the interaction rule.
- The same input previously worked in another mode: compare mode indicators and preconditions. Suspend transfer of the rule instead of rejecting all earlier evidence.

Keep the resulting diagnosis narrow: which assumption, what observation, what alternative, and what would discriminate it. Avoid claiming a software/transport failure from a valid but surprising game frame.
